# Patient-dog identity

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

The patient-dog is the animal in care. Typical US daycare and boarding desks keep a separate identity from the client so feeding cards, vaccine-file rows, crate or suite assignments, and incident reports do not blend two dogs in one household. Most shops store a kennel name, a software ID, color or markings as typed, and sometimes a photo filename. Use their identifiers exactly as exported. Two dogs arriving in one car are two rows even when they share a client and a boarding date. A daycare pack and an overnight board for the same dog are still one patient-dog with two stay types if their file splits that way. You do not invent a kennel name to tidy a list. You do not merge two charts because the names look alike. Chip and license numbers, when present, copy as written; they are identifiers, not a registry login you run from this folder. This is not a veterinary medical-record number you interpret, and you do not open a clinic chart. If the drop-off note says “the dog” and the household has two, ask which patient-dog. Starter / guess until they teach how *this* shop labels dogs on the floor board versus in the software. Do not present this note as how this kennel already prints wristbands or cards.

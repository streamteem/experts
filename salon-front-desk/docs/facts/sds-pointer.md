# SDS / chemical files

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

OSHA-oriented practice keeps safety data sheets available for products that may be hazardous—color, acrylic liquids, disinfectants, and similar. You may point at their SDS folder or list product names they already store. You do not diagnose a chemical exposure or tell them a product is “safe.” Desks keep a pointer so a question about a bottle goes to the sheet they already filed, not to your opinion. Typical files are a binder photo, a vendor SDS PDF folder, or a list of product names. OSHA hair-salon and nail-salon chemical pages are orientation. You do not rewrite an SDS. You do not say the shop meets OSHA. If a product on the backbar has no SDS in their folder, list the name as missing from the folder—not as a violation finding. Starter until they teach where this shop keeps SDS files.

# Duration and buffer

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A color or foil service often needs chair time plus processing time when the guest is not in the chair—the stylist may take another guest or an assistant may rinse. Typical books store that as duration plus a buffer, as two linked services, or as a processing block that looks empty but is not free. Desks keep the buffer so a walk-in is not seated into wet color. Do not collapse a thirty-minute process gap into an open walk-in slot unless their export marks the gap as free. Slot length comes from the service setup or the stylist’s note, not from a guess that “color is always two hours.” Correction and lightening usually need more buffer than a gloss. If the menu and the book disagree on minutes, quote both and ask. You do not shorten a buffer to make the afternoon look open. Starter until they teach how this floor codes process time.

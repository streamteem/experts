# Target pest as their label, not a diagnosis

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Target pest is the word they already put on the ticket, graph, or sales sheet: ants, roaches, rodents, termites, bed bugs, bees, wildlife as they labeled it, or “general pest.” Typical US pest offices treat that label as the sold scope, not as an entomology finding you write. Copy their words. Do not upgrade “bugs in the kitchen” to a species. Do not diagnose a structure, a moisture problem, or a colony as the cause. CDC and PestWorld pages are orientation only. If the ticket has no target label, ask; do not fill one so the chemical log looks complete. A landscaping “treat weeds” note is not this field. Wildlife and WDO stay on their own forms when they split them. You list the label they stored. They inspect and they choose the service. Starter until they teach how *this* shop codes general-pest versus specialty tickets and who may change a target label after a visit.

# NSF, returned payments, chargebacks

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a bounced customer check or returned ACH reverses the deposit and may add a bank fee. The invoice often reopens. A card chargeback is similar: the processor pulls money and the sale is disputed. These events exist so cash and AR stay honest. Next files are the bank line, the processor notice, and the AR questions pack. Typical fields are original payment date, return date, customer, amount, fee, and invoice number if known. Files are the bank statement, processor email, and the invoice. Cadence is when it happens and at month-end if AR looks odd. The bookkeeper flags and proposes the reverse; the owner collects again. Shops vary on whether they bill the NSF fee to the customer. Ask their steps. A starter guess until they teach: do not delete the original invoice; list the return as a question. Common mistakes are deleting the invoice to hide it, recoding a chargeback as a vendor bill, and expensing the whole sale. Never invent a fee billed to the customer, never decide the dispute as a lawyer, never write off without their word, and never treat a chargeback as cost of goods.

# Processor fees and net deposits

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk processor activity is Square, Stripe, PayPal, Clover, or a merchant account: customers pay, the processor takes fees, refunds, and chargebacks, and a net payout hits the bank. Books that only record the net deposit understate sales and miss fee expense, and they can miss sales tax on gross if the shop collects tax. The payout report exists so gross, fees, and net can be tied to the bank. Next files are the merchant-payout match and the sales-tax list if they collect. Typical fields are payout date, gross charges, refunds, fees, net, and payout id. Files are the processor payout CSV or PDF they export. Cadence is each payout and monthly. The bookkeeper ties net to the bank; they export. No dashboard login here. Shops vary. Some record gross sales plus fees; some live with net in income. Ask. Do not invent fee percentages. A starter guess until they teach: match net to bank and list fees as a question until they teach gross versus net. Common mistakes are coding the bank net as sales and stopping, double-counting POS sales plus the payout, and inventing a fee percent. Never invent fees, never file a 1099-K question as settled, never store processor passwords, and never treat a blog threshold as this shop's 1099-K rule.

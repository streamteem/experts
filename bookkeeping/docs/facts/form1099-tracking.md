# 1099 tracking as files

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk 1099 work is a year-end file: vendors, year-to-date service payments, whether a W-9 is on file, entity type as they recorded, and whether they think the payee is reportable. It exists so they or their CPA can file from a clean list, not from memory. Next files are the vendor-YTD CSV, the W-9 present-or-missing list, and the tracker write-up. Thresholds and form types change. Read the current IRS 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC instructions they care about. Do not quote a threshold as this shop's rule unless they taught it. Typical fields are payee label, YTD amount, W-9 path yes or no, form they think applies, and notes. No taxpayer ID numbers. Files are a spreadsheet they maintain or you build from their vendor payments. Cadence is quarterly for some shops, year-end for most. The bookkeeper tracks; they or their CPA file. Shops vary on who is exempt, who is product-only, and who uses a corporation. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: list service payees and W-9 gaps; leave reportable as a question. Common mistakes are pasting TINs into the sheet, treating a blog dollar amount as law for this shop, and e-filing from this folder. Never e-file, never decide who is exempt, never put TINs in docs/, never give a classification opinion, and never say forms are filed.

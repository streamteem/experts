# Business credit cards

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a business credit card is a liability account, not a bank. Charges are expenses, job costs, or personal questions; the payment from the operating bank is a transfer that reduces the card, not a second expense. The card exists so the owner can float purchases and so the books can show what is still owed to the card issuer. Next files are the card-coding pack, the card rec, and the bank line for the payment. Typical fields are transaction date, payee, amount, category, statement ending date, statement ending balance, and payment date. Files are the statement PDF plus a CSV if they have it. Cadence is monthly per card, sometimes weekly. The bookkeeper codes and recs; they download. Ask every card they use, including store cards. Shops vary on personal charges (draw versus reimbursement versus "leave it"). Ask. A starter guess until they teach: code the statement period they named; treat the bank payment as a transfer; flag personal-looking lines as questions. Common mistakes are expensing the payment and the charges, treating the card as a checking account, and skipping store cards. Never invent a personal-versus-business call, never store a full card number, never pay the card, never log into the issuer, and never treat a starter "all business" guess as their policy.

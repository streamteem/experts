# Accruals as optional

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk an accrual is a month-end entry for a known cost or amount earned that is not yet on a bill or invoice: unpaid wages, unpaid commissions, or a vendor invoice that will arrive next month. It exists so an accrual-method profit and loss matches the period. Many small shops skip this. Next files are the close pack and any reversing journals they already use. Typical fields are date, account, amount, memo, and reverse date if they reverse next month. Files are a payroll cutoff note, a vendor email, or their close checklist. Cadence is month-end only if they use accruals. The bookkeeper drafts only after they say to; the CPA may require it at year end only. Shops vary. Cash shops usually do not accrue. Month-end "accrue then reverse" is a choice they or their CPA make. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: no accruals until their close list says so. Common mistakes are adding accruals to make a cash-basis profit and loss look like large-company books, accruing a guess with no vendor file, and leaving last month's accrual unreversed. Never invent amounts, never add surprise accruals, never present an accrual as a tax method change, and never hide a missing bill inside an accrual instead of asking.

# Operating expense

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk operating expense is the overhead that keeps the shop running: rent, utilities, insurance, software, ads, office, professional fees, and repairs they already treat as expense. It exists so the profit and loss can show what it costs to operate apart from product or job cost and apart from owner draws. Next files are coded bills, card coding, and the close P&L questions. Typical fields are date, payee, amount, account name from their chart, class or location if they use it, and a receipt path. Files are bills, card lines, and the chart export. Cadence is weekly coding and monthly review. The bookkeeper proposes accounts; the owner corrects names. Shops vary on account names and on what they capitalize. Mapping to Schedule C lines is a CPA handoff, not this desk's opinion. Ask their account names. A starter guess until they teach: use the closest name on their chart and label it a guess if you are not sure. Common mistakes are recoding a capitalized purchase as office expense because it is under a round number you invented, hiding owner personal charges in supplies, and creating new expense names instead of asking. Never invent a capitalization threshold, never apply a meals percentage "for the IRS," never give a deductibility opinion, and never pay a bill.

# Petty cash and cash on hand

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk petty cash is a small box or envelope with a log and occasional replenish from the operating account. A retail till or cash drawer is a different count tied to the day's sales. The account exists so small cash spends have a home and so unidentified cash is not parked there. Next files are the petty-cash log and the replenish check or withdrawal. Typical fields are date, amount, payee or purpose, and remaining cash if they count. Files are a log sheet and receipts. Cadence is when the box is low, or daily for a till. The bookkeeper recs the log if they use one; a cashier or owner handles cash. Shops vary. Many shops use no cash. Ask whether this shop uses cash at all. A starter guess until they teach: do not create a petty-cash account to park unidentified cash. Common mistakes are using petty cash as a suspense dump, income-coding a cash deposit as owner because it was cash, and skipping till shorts. Short or over goes to their account if they have one. Never invent a till count, never take or replenish cash, never store a safe combination, and never treat missing cash as a tax deduction.

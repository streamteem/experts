# Source documents

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk source documents are the papers and files that back a books line: vendor PDFs, bank and card CSVs, processor payouts, payroll registers, and photos of paper receipts. They exist because IRS recordkeeping orientation is that supporting documents back the books, and because the owner and CPA will ask "what was this?" months later. Next files are the receipt-match pack, coded bills, and the close questions list. Typical index fields are date, vendor as printed, amount, tax if shown, last four of a card if already printed, and filename. Files live in a drop folder they name, email, or a capture-app export. Cadence is daily drop and weekly match. The bookkeeper matches; the owner supplies missing photos. Shops vary on what is "enough" support under a dollar threshold and where files land. Ask where this shop drops files and what they treat as enough for a small line. A starter guess until they teach: card lines they care about want a file; still ask before treating a chat recap as the source. Common mistakes are coding from memory, keeping only a chat summary, and photographing a full card number. IRS Publication 583 is orientation only. Never invent an amount or vendor when the file is missing, never store passwords, never copy a full card number into docs/, and never say a missing receipt is "fine for the IRS" as a tax opinion.

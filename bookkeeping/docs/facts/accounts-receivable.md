# Accounts receivable

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk accounts receivable is unpaid customer invoices: who owes the shop, which invoice, and how old. It exists so collections, deposits, and the cash forecast are not guesswork. Next files are the AR aging export, cash-application questions, and the AR write-up. This Expert does not write off balances and does not email customers unless they asked and the list is theirs. Typical fields are customer, invoice number, invoice date, due date, original amount, open amount, and bucket. Files are the software export or a spreadsheet they keep. Cadence is weekly for collections, monthly for close. The bookkeeper ages and flags; the owner collects. Shops vary on aging by invoice date versus due date and on how payments apply (oldest open versus invoice number in the memo). Ask their cash-application rule. A starter guess until they teach: age with their export's buckets; do not apply a payment because the amount matches. Common mistakes are applying a payment to the wrong invoice, writing off to make aging pretty, and calling a credit in 90-plus uncollectible. Never invent a write-off, never decide bad-debt tax treatment, never store card numbers from a customer, and never threaten a customer as counsel.

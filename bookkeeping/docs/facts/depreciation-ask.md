# Depreciation — ask, do not compute as tax

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk depreciation is the entry that expenses part of a fixed asset over time on the books. Book depreciation and tax depreciation (section 179, bonus) often differ. This desk may list assets and ask the CPA for the entry. The fact exists so the close pack does not invent a pretty number. Next files are the asset list and a question in the year-end handoff. IRS Publication 946 is orientation for them and their CPA, not a worksheet you complete as tax. Typical fields you may list are asset, cost, date, and "depreciation entry: ask CPA." Files are the asset list and any prior-year entry they already posted. Cadence is year-end, sometimes monthly if they already have a book schedule. The bookkeeper asks; the CPA computes tax. Shops vary. Many small shops only book what the CPA sends after the return. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: no depreciation number until their CPA file or last-year entry is in the folder. Common mistakes are inventing a monthly amount so the P&L "looks right," mixing book and tax, and saying a write-off is allowed. Never compute tax depreciation, never say 179 or bonus is allowed, never invent a life, and never present a starter schedule as theirs.

# Owner draws

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk an owner draw is money the owner takes for personal use, or a personal charge on a business card that they treat as personal. It is an equity reduction (draw or distribution), not an operating expense and not cost of goods. It exists so the profit and loss stays about the shop and the balance sheet shows what the owner took. Next files are the bank or card coding pack, the equity rollforward, and the CPA's year-end questions about basis and distributions. This Expert does not compute basis or say a distribution is allowed. Typical fields are date, amount, the equity account they use, and a memo. Files are the bank line, card line, or check image. Cadence is whenever cash leaves for the owner. The bookkeeper codes; the owner confirms what was personal. Shops vary. Entity types use different equity labels. S-corp owner wages, if they run payroll, are a different file. Do not recode a draw as wages. Ask how this shop labels owner cash, personal card charges, and year-end distributions. A starter guess until they teach: personal-looking charges are questions, not silent draws. Common mistakes are dumping draws into meals, auto, or cost of goods to "make the P&L look right," and treating a distribution as wages without their payroll file. Never invent their entity type, never give a tax opinion on reasonable compensation, never pay the owner, and never store account passwords.

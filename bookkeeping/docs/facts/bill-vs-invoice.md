# Bill vs invoice

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a bill is what the shop owes a vendor (accounts payable). An invoice is what a customer owes the shop (accounts receivable). The split exists so AP aging, the pay list, AR aging, and cash application do not land in the same pile. Next files are the vendor-bills pack, the AR questions pack, and the bank rec when payments clear. Typical fields on a bill are vendor, date, due date, amount, account or category, and bill number. Typical fields on an invoice are customer, date, due date, amount, items or a memo, and invoice number. Files are vendor PDFs, customer PDFs, and software exports. Cadence is daily or weekly as paper and email arrive. The bookkeeper enters; the owner approves payment and customer terms. Shops vary. Some owners say invoice for both. Some cash shops never enter bills and only code the bank when paid. Ask which words this shop uses and whether they enter AP at all. A starter guess until they teach: a PDF from a supplier is a bill; a PDF they send a customer is an invoice. Common mistakes are posting the same PDF as both a bill and an invoice, entering a customer estimate as an invoice, and paying from a quote. Do not post the same PDF as both unless their file shows a pass-through. Never invent a named customer's terms, never send payment, and never invent a tax treatment for a pass-through.

# Fiscal year vs calendar

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk the income-tax year is often the calendar year, but some shops use a fiscal year that ends in another month. Sales-tax years and payroll calendar years may differ from the income-tax year. The year-end date exists so YTD 1099 totals, close packs, and "this year versus last" mean the same thing. Next files are the close calendar and the 1099 tracker year they name. Typical fields are year-end month and day, and labels for sales-tax and payroll years if they differ. Files are a CPA email or the software company settings they export. Cadence is once, then confirmed before year-end work. The bookkeeper asks; they or the CPA set it. Shops vary. Do not assume December. Ask this shop's year-end before building a YTD 1099 or a close pack. A starter guess until they teach: calendar year until they say otherwise, labeled as a guess. Common mistakes are building 1099 YTD on a calendar year for a fiscal shop, mixing sales-tax quarters with the wrong income year, and closing "December" when they are fiscal. Never invent a year-end, never change their software year, never give a fiscal-year tax opinion, and never assume a starter calendar year is filed.

# Billable expenses

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a billable expense is a cost the shop paid (parts, permit, sub, travel) that they intend to put on a customer invoice. Software may mark a bill line billable. It exists so job cost and AR stay tied and so the owner does not forget to bill. Next files are the job-cost sheet, the customer invoice draft they asked for, and AP. Typical fields are job or customer, vendor, amount, billed-or-not, and the income or reimbursement item they use when billed. Files are the bill PDF and their invoice. Cadence is weekly as jobs run. The bookkeeper flags unbilled; they invoice. You do not send the customer invoice unless they said that is the job and the file is theirs. Shops vary. Some markup; some bill at cost. Ask their rule. Do not invent a markup. A starter guess until they teach: tag the cost to their job if they use jobs; leave markup as a question. Common mistakes are inventing a markup percent, forgetting to mark billed so it double-bills, and putting owner draws on a customer invoice. Never invent markup, never invent a job, never treat billable as a tax pass-through opinion, and never send an invoice from this folder unless they asked and the list is theirs.

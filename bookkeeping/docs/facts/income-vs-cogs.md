# Income vs COGS

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk income is what customers were charged for the shop's work or goods (plus contra-revenue such as refunds). Cost of goods is the material, product, or subcontract cost that moves with a sale. The split exists so gross profit is readable and so the CPA is not handed a pile of "job stuff" mixed with rent. Next files are the coded-bills pack, job-cost sheet if they use one, and the P&L the owner reviews. Typical fields are account name, amount, customer or job if they tag it, and vendor. Files are invoices, bills, and the chart. Cadence is every sales and purchase batch. The bookkeeper codes; the owner says what is a job cost versus overhead. Shops vary. Job shops may use job costing instead of a formal cost-of-goods account. Retail may use inventory and a cost adjustment. Service shops may have no cost of goods. Ask this shop's split. A starter guess until they teach: customer invoices to income; vendor product or sub bills that they already treat as job cost to cost of goods; rent and owner draws elsewhere. Common mistakes are dumping all vendor bills into cost of goods because they "feel like jobs," putting owner draws in cost of goods, and netting refunds against a random expense. Never invent their cost-of-goods policy, never map to a tax form line as an opinion, and never recode their chart because a textbook used different names.

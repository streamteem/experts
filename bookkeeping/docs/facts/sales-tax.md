# Sales tax — ask, do not invent

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk sales tax is the money invoiced or collected on taxable sales, plus the liability and remittance, as this shop already tracks it on a sheet or in software. It is state and local. It exists so the owner can see what was collected and what they still owe, and so their CPA or sales-tax filer has a list. Next files are the sales-tax questions pack and the payable tie-out. This Expert does not file a return. Typical fields are invoice date, customer or channel, taxable amount as they marked, tax collected, and jurisdiction only if their file already has it. Files are invoice or POS exports and their working sheet. Cadence follows their filing period, which you ask and never invent. The bookkeeper lists; they or their CPA file. Shops vary on nexus, item taxability, marketplace facilitator rules, and rates. Streamlined Sales Tax orientation does not give you a rate for this shop. Ask their nexus list, item taxability, and who files. A starter guess until they teach: list tax collected as marked on their invoices; leave rate and nexus as questions. Common mistakes are inventing a rate from a city name, treating marketplace payouts as if the shop collected tax, and writing off a leftover liability. Never invent a rate or nexus, never say a return is filed, never pick a jurisdiction because a customer address looks local, and never give a tax opinion.

# Fixed assets and receipts for equipment

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a fixed-asset list is lasting equipment, vehicles, and build-outs: date placed, cost, vendor, and a short description. It exists so a truck is not lost in repairs expense and so the CPA has a list for depreciation. Next files are the receipt, the loan if any, and the depreciation ask. Whether a purchase is a repair expense or an asset is often a CPA call. Typical fields are asset label, date, cost, vendor, account they use, and notes. Files are a spreadsheet plus invoices. Cadence is when a large purchase arrives and at year-end. The bookkeeper lists; the CPA decides tax depreciation. Shops vary on a dollar threshold. Ask this shop's threshold if they have one. A starter guess until they teach: list the invoice and ask capital versus expense; do not invent a policy. Common mistakes are inventing a capitalization policy, inventing a useful life, and expensing a vehicle because the owner "wants the write-off." Never invent policy or life, never compute section 179 or bonus, never say a write-off is allowed, and never create asset accounts for a named company without their chart.

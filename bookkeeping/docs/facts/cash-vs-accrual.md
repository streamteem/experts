# Cash vs accrual

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk, cash versus accrual is the shop's books method: when income and expenses hit the registers, not a tax election this Expert may choose. Cash books record income when received and expenses when paid. Accrual books record income when earned and expenses when the bill is owed, even if unpaid. The method exists so the profit and loss, aging, bank rec, and year-end pack the owner hands their CPA tell one story. IRS Publication 334 orientation is that books should clearly show income and use the same method as the return. This Expert does not pick the method, say a change is allowed, or file anything. Typical files: a close-checklist note, the software company setting, or a CPA email. Cadence is always. The owner, bookkeeper, and CPA touch it; only they may change it. Shops vary. Mixed or modified-cash books are common. Inventory shops may treat purchases and sales differently from the rest of the P&L. Ask which this shop uses for the operating bank, cards, and any inventory. A starter guess until they teach: many small service shops run cash on the bank and still enter customer invoices. Do not assume that is this shop. Common mistakes: coding unpaid bills as expenses on cash books, or skipping receivables on accrual books because the customer has not paid. Never invent a method for a named company, never advise a method change as a tax position, and never treat a starter note as their filed return method.

# Sales tax payable on the books

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk sales-tax payable is the liability for tax collected (or accrued) until they remit. Typical accrual: tax on invoices increases the liability; the remittance reduces it. The account exists so the balance is explainable from the period's taxable sales, not a random leftover. Next files are their sales-tax report and the remittance bank line. You do not file the return. Typical fields are period, tax collected as their report shows, remittance date and amount, and remaining liability. Files are the software sales-tax report or their sheet plus the bank draft. Cadence is each filing period they already use. The bookkeeper ties; they or their CPA file. Shops vary on cash versus accrual for the liability and on marketplace-collected tax. Ask their report. A starter guess until they teach: list collected and remitted from their files; leftover after they say they paid is a question, not a write-off to income. Common mistakes are writing off leftover to income, inventing a remittance, and treating marketplace tax as theirs when their file says the platform remitted. Never invent a rate, never say a return is filed, never remit, and never give a nexus opinion.

# Vendor credits

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a vendor credit is money the supplier owes the shop: a return, a pricing error, or a rebate sitting on AP as a credit memo, not as a random negative bill if they use credit memos. It exists so returns reduce what they pay and so aging is not a mystery credit in 90-plus. Next files are the AP aging, the pay list (net of credits), and the vendor-bills pack. Typical fields are vendor, credit date, original bill if any, amount, and whether it is applied or still open. Files are the credit PDF and the AP export. Cadence is when credits arrive and at each pay run. The bookkeeper applies if that is their job; the owner decides which open bill to net. Shops vary. Some take credits as a check; some only net on the next payment. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: list open credits on the pay list and ask before applying. Common mistakes are expensing a vendor credit as income, leaving credits unapplied, and entering a negative bill when they use credit memos. Never invent a credit, never take a rebate as owner cash, never treat a credit as taxable income as an opinion, and never pay a net amount from this folder.

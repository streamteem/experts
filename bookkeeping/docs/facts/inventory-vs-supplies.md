# Inventory vs supplies

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk inventory is goods held for sale or for jobs that will be billed; incidental shop supplies are often expensed when bought. The split exists so the balance sheet and cost of goods are not a junk drawer of rags and resale parts. Next files are their count sheet if they count, the parts-house statement, and the CPA's inventory questions. IRS Publication 334 discusses inventory and small-business exceptions. This Expert does not decide the tax method. Typical fields are item or a simple description, quantity if they count, unit cost if they store it, and the asset or expense account they use. Files are a count spreadsheet, a parts-house export, or "we expense everything" in their close notes. Cadence is year-end for many shops, monthly for some retailers. The bookkeeper lists; they count. Shops vary. Some expense everything. Some keep a perpetual parts list. Ask whether this shop counts stock, uses a parts house on account, or expenses everything. A starter guess until they teach: treat as supplies expense until their file shows an inventory asset. Common mistakes are inventing a count, inventing a unit cost, and building a fake inventory asset so the books "look accrual." Never invent quantities or costs, never choose a tax inventory method, never write off shrinkage as a tax opinion, and never create inventory accounts for a named company without their chart.

# Repair vs improvement — ask

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a repair is routine fix-up they already expense; an improvement or restoration may be an asset. The line is a common CPA question. IRS Publication 535 is orientation, not a decision tree you apply as tax. This desk lists the vendor, amount, and what the invoice describes. The distinction exists so a roof or a remodel does not silently sit in repairs. Next files are the invoice, the asset list, and a CPA question. Typical fields are date, vendor, amount, invoice description copied as written, and "ask CPA: capital vs repair." Files are the vendor PDF and photos if they have them. Cadence is when a large facilities or equipment invoice arrives, and at year-end review. The bookkeeper lists; the CPA decides tax treatment. Shops vary on dollar thresholds and on splitting a project across invoices. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: expense only if they already treat that vendor as repairs; otherwise list and ask. Common mistakes are deciding capital versus repair for tax, splitting a project across years to "help" them, and inventing a betterment test. Never decide for tax, never invent a split to change a year, never compute depreciation, and never treat Publication 535 as a filing position for this shop.

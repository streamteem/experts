# Double-entry sketch

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk double-entry is how the software keeps score even when the owner never sees the words debit and credit: every bill, invoice, payment, and journal has a balancing side. It exists so the trial balance stays in balance and so a CSV import that lands only one side is caught before the CPA pack. Next files are the journal proposal (only if they allow it) and the trial-balance questions. Typical fields on a journal are date, accounts, debit amount, credit amount, memo, and period. Files are a journal CSV or a screenshot of a transaction. Cadence is as needed, plus month-end if they use standard journals. The bookkeeper proposes; they post in their software unless they asked you only to draft. Shops vary. Some shops allow proposed journals; others want bank lines coded only. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: code bank and bills; do not post a one-sided adjustment. Common mistakes are a one-sided "adjustment" to hide a mismatch, importing a register with no offset, and using miscellaneous expense as the silent other side. Never invent a balancing account to make a pack look clean, never give a tax opinion via a journal description, never plug a rec, and never store a software password to post it yourself.

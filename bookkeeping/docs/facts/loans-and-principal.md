# Loans and principal

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a loan is a liability: truck, equipment, EIDL, line of credit, or a recorded note. Each payment usually splits interest (expense) and principal (reduces the loan). New proceeds are cash in and more liability, not sales. The split exists so the profit and loss is not overstated and so the loan does not sit forever at the original amount. Next files are the lender statement, the payment split worksheet, and the balance-sheet tie-out. Typical fields are payment date, total paid, interest, principal, fees if shown, and remaining balance as the lender states. Files are the amortization or monthly lender statement they provide. Cadence is each payment and at month-end for the rec of any loan account. The bookkeeper splits from their file; they get the statement. Shops vary on extra principal, interest-only periods, and whether a finance company for insurance is treated as a small loan. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: do not split until the lender file is in the folder; leave the full payment as a question. Common mistakes are expensing the full payment, booking proceeds as income, and guessing the split from a leftover liability. Never guess the split, never invent an interest rate, never give a debt-tax opinion, never contact the lender as them, and never pay the loan from this folder.

# Job costing (light)

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US trade or remodel desk job costing is a tag on the same books: bills, materials, and sometimes labor coded to a job number so the owner can see job profit. It is not a second set of books. It exists so the owner can decide what to bid next and so the close pack can show open-job cost. Next files are the job-cost sheet and the job write-up. Typical fields are job id as they already use it, customer, vendor, date, amount, category, and optionally hours if they track labor. Files are their job list, bills, and payroll or sub bills. Cadence is weekly as bills arrive and at job close. The bookkeeper tags; the owner owns the job list and any overhead rule. Shops vary. Owner time is often not costed. Overhead allocation may not exist. Ask their job list and whether labor is payroll, owner time, or sub bills. A starter guess until they teach: tag only when the bill already shows a job or they named it; leave untagged as a question. Common mistakes are inventing a job number, allocating overhead with a rate they did not give, and putting owner draws on a job as "labor." Never invent jobs, never invent an overhead rate, never treat job profit as a tax return, and never pay a sub from this folder.

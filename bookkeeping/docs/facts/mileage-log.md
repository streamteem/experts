# Mileage and vehicle logs

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk vehicle support is a mileage log or a written allowance policy: dates, miles, and business purpose, or odometer readings they already keep. It exists so vehicle costs on the books have a file behind them when the owner or CPA asks. Next files are the log export and the vehicle-cost questions. Cents-per-mile versus actual expenses is a tax method. Ask their CPA path. Do not pick. Typical fields are date, miles or start and end odometer, purpose as they wrote, and vehicle label. Files are a spreadsheet, app export, or paper scan. Cadence is monthly or at year-end. The bookkeeper lists what the log shows; they drive and write the log. Shops vary. Some use a flat reimbursement. Some put everything on a shop truck card and skip miles. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: list fuel and repairs from the card; do not invent miles. Common mistakes are inventing miles from a calendar, applying a standard mileage rate they did not teach as their book method, and putting a personal car loan on the business books. Never invent miles, never pick the tax method, never compute a deduction, and never store a driver's license number.

# Prepaid and deposits paid

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a prepaid is a payment that covers future months (insurance, software, rent paid ahead) sitting on the balance sheet until it is expensed. A deposit paid to a landlord or vendor may sit as an asset until applied. It exists so a large cash outlay does not smash one month's profit and loss unless that is how they already book. Next files are their prepaid schedule and the month-end journals they already use. Typical fields are vendor, payment date, coverage start and end if on the invoice, monthly amount if they amortize, and the asset and expense accounts they named. Files are the invoice plus their schedule. Cadence is when the invoice arrives and then monthly if they amortize. The bookkeeper follows their schedule; the CPA may set the method. Shops vary. Cash shops often expense when paid and never use prepaid. Accrual shops may. Ask whether this shop uses prepaid at all. A starter guess until they teach: expense when paid unless their file already has a prepaid schedule. Common mistakes are inventing a twelve-month amortization for a modest annual app, forgetting to expense the last month, and calling a refundable deposit an expense. Never invent an amortization schedule, never treat prepaid as a tax election, never guess a useful life, and never build prepaid to make a cash-basis P&L look like a textbook.

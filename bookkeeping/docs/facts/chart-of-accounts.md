# Chart of accounts

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk the chart of accounts is the live list of income, cost of goods, operating expense, other income and expense, assets, liabilities, and equity names the software posts to. It exists so every coded bill, bank line, invoice, and journal lands in a bucket the owner and their CPA can read on a profit and loss or balance sheet. Next files are the coded bills pack, the trial balance, and the year-end handoff. Typical fields are account name, type or family, sometimes a number, and active or hidden. Files are a software export, PDF, or screenshot. Cadence is whenever they add a vendor type or the CPA asks for a new split. The bookkeeper proposes; the owner or CPA approves a new name. Shops vary. Software ships a generic list; shops rename, number, merge, and hide. Ask for this shop's export of the live chart. A starter sketch in this folder is only a guess until they teach. Never publish a numbered chart as if it already belonged to a named company. Common mistakes are merging two of their accounts because the labels look similar, inventing "Office expense" when they already use "Supplies," and posting owner draws to an expense family. Never invent a chart for a named company, never choose their numbering, never map books to Schedule C as a tax opinion, and never store a login to pull the list. Wait for their file.

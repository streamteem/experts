# Customer deposits and retainers

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a customer deposit or retainer is money received before the work is done or before it is earned under their rule. Accrual books often sit it as a liability (unearned or customer deposit) and move it to income when earned. Cash shops may income it when received. The distinction exists so a busy deposit month is not fake sales. Next files are the invoice when they apply it and the AR or liability detail. Typical fields are date received, customer, amount, job if any, and the liability or income account they use. Files are the receipt, the estimate, and their rule. Cadence is when money arrives and when the invoice applies it. The bookkeeper asks; they decide earned. Shops vary. Ask this shop. A starter guess until they teach: do not call a deposit sales on accrual-looking books without their rule; list it as a question. Common mistakes are income-coding every deposit, spending-coding a deposit as the owner's, and applying a deposit to the wrong job. Never invent their earned rule, never give a constructive-receipt opinion, never move money, and never treat a retainer as a gift card unless they said so.

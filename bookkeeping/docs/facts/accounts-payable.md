# Accounts payable

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk accounts payable is the list of vendor bills not yet paid: who is owed, how much, and when. Accrual shops enter the bill when received. Cash shops may never put AP on the books and only code the bank when paid. The list exists so the owner can pay on purpose and so aging is not a pile of email. Next files are the AP aging export, the pay list, and the vendor-bills pack. They pay. You do not. Typical fields are vendor, bill date, due date, amount, account, status (open or paid), and bill number. Files are the software aging or a folder of unpaid PDFs. Cadence is weekly for a pay run, monthly for close. The bookkeeper enters and ages; the owner chooses what to pay. Shops vary. A cash shop with no AP register still has unpaid PDFs. List those as open bills for the owner, labeled not on the books if that is true. Ask whether they enter bills or only code the bank. A starter guess until they teach: enter bills if they already do; otherwise build a pay list from PDFs and label it. Common mistakes are paying from an estimate, entering every purchase order as AP, and coding a card charge as a bill and again as a card expense. Never invent a due date, never send payment, never invent terms, and never treat the pay list as filed with anyone.

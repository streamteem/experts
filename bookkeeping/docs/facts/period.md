# Period and close

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk the books period is the slice of time a pack covers: usually a calendar month, sometimes a week for AP, sometimes a 4-4-5 or fiscal month. It exists so coded bills, bank recs, aging, and close notes can be compared to the same statement and the same profit and loss. Next files are every work CSV for that pack and the month-end write-up. Typical fields are period start, period end, and a label (March 2026, week ending date). Files are the close calendar they keep or a one-line instruction. Cadence is the close itself. The bookkeeper asks first; the owner or CPA sets lock dates. Shops vary. Some code AP weekly and close books monthly. Some use a fiscal year that is not December. Ask the period before coding a batch. Ask what locked means here: a software close date versus a CPA review. A starter guess until they teach: one calendar month matching the bank statement dates they handed you. Common mistakes are mixing two months in one work file, using a rolling 90-day bank download as "the month," and editing a locked period to hide a miss. Never invent their fiscal year, never unlock their software, never treat a starter calendar month as their year-end, and never call a period closed if they did not accept the rec.

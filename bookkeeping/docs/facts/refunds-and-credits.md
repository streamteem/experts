# Refunds and credit memos

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk a credit memo reduces what a customer owes. A refund check or card credit reduces cash and either applies that credit or hits a contra-revenue account. Vendor credits work the other way on AP. The trail exists so returns do not disappear into supplies. Next files are the credit memo, the bank or processor line, and AR or AP aging. Typical fields are date, customer or vendor, original invoice or bill, credit amount, and whether cash was refunded. Files are the credit PDF and the processor or bank line. Cadence is when returns happen. The bookkeeper applies; the owner approves refunds. You do not send money. Shops vary. Some enter a negative invoice; some require credit memos. Ask their path. A starter guess until they teach: do not enter a negative invoice if their file shows credit memos. Common mistakes are expensing a customer refund as supplies, leaving credits unapplied in 90-plus aging, and refunding in the books without their payment. Never invent a credit, never pay a refund, never decide restocking as a legal term, and never treat a refund as a tax-free event as an opinion.

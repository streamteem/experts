# Payment terms

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk payment terms are the due-date rule printed on a vendor bill or a customer invoice: due on receipt, net 10, net 15, net 30, or an early-pay discount such as 2/10 net 30. Terms exist so aging buckets, the pay list, and cash-flow questions match what the paper actually says. Next files are AP and AR aging and the owner's "what do we pay this Friday" list. Typical fields are the terms label, invoice or bill date, due date, discount date if they use one, and discount amount if they take it. Files are the PDF header, the vendor or customer record in their software, and sometimes a rate card. Cadence is per document. The bookkeeper copies what is on the page; the owner decides whether to take a discount or pay late. Shops vary. Early-pay discounts and late fees are their policy, not a starter rule. Some shops ignore printed terms and pay on a fixed weekday. Ask this shop's rule and do not invent a named customer's terms. A starter guess until they teach: use the due date on the PDF, and flag if the software due date disagrees. Common mistakes are changing a due date to make aging look prettier, assuming everyone is net 30, and booking a discount they did not take. Never invent late-fee income, never decide they "should" take 2/10, and never treat a starter net-30 as this shop's contract.

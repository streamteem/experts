# Balance-sheet families

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk the balance-sheet families are assets (bank, receivables, inventory, prepaid, fixed assets), liabilities (payables, cards, loans, sales-tax payable, payroll liabilities), and equity (contributions, draws, retained earnings). Even a shop that only looks at a profit and loss has these under the hood. They exist so cash, debt, and owner activity are not dumped into income. Next files are the rec pack, loan split, and year-end trial balance. Typical fields are account name, family, and balance as of the period end. Files are a trial-balance or balance-sheet export. Cadence is monthly in a close, or whenever a new loan or account appears. The bookkeeper lists what the export shows; the owner confirms new accounts. Shops vary on which balance-sheet accounts they actually use. Many cash shops have little AR or AP on the books. Ask. A starter guess until they teach: bank, card liability, and owner equity at minimum; still wait for their chart. Common mistakes are creating a loan account for a named lender without their chart, calling a card a bank, and booking owner contributions as sales. Never invent loan or asset accounts for a named company, never decide depreciation, never give a solvency or legal opinion, and never treat a starter family list as their chart.

# Check register and outstanding checks

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk the check register is handwritten or printed checks, plus voids, that may not look like a card swipe on the bank feed. The bank later shows the check number and amount. The register exists so outstanding checks can be listed on the rec and so a missing check is a question. Next files are the outstanding-checks list and the bank rec. Typical fields are check number, date, payee, amount, account, and cleared-or-outstanding. Files are a check-stub export, a written register photo, or the software check list. Cadence is whenever they write checks and at statement rec. The bookkeeper lists outstanding; they sign checks. You do not sign or mail. Shops vary. Many shops are ACH-only. Ask if they still write checks. A starter guess until they teach: if the statement shows check numbers, ask for the register; do not invent payees from amounts. Common mistakes are coding a cleared check as a new expense when the bill was already entered, leaving old outstanding checks forever, and treating a void as still outstanding. Never sign or mail a check, never invent a payee, never store a signature image, and never originate ACH because a check is late.

# Vehicle costs

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk vehicle costs are fuel, repairs, insurance, and loan payments on a shop truck or car they already treat as business. Loan principal is not fuel. Mixed personal use is an ask. The pile exists so auto is not mixed with owner draws and so the loan split stays correct. Next files are the card coding, the loan statement, and the mileage log if they keep one. Typical fields are date, payee, amount, vehicle label if they have more than one, and personal-use question if mixed. Files are card lines, fuel receipts, and the loan file. Cadence is monthly. The bookkeeper codes; they assign vehicles. Shops vary. Do not apply a standard mileage rate unless they taught that as their book method. A starter guess until they teach: code fuel and repairs to their auto accounts; leave mixed use and mileage method as questions. Common mistakes are putting a personal car loan on the business balance sheet without their word, expensing loan principal as fuel, and applying a cents-per-mile rate they did not teach. Never invent personal-use percent, never pick the tax method, never compute a deduction, and never store a VIN in a public write-up unless their file already uses it as a label.

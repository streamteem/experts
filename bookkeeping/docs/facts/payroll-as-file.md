# Payroll as a file, not a filing

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

On a typical US desk payroll arrives as a register file from a service or in-house software: gross wages, taxes withheld, employer taxes, net pay, and the cash leaving the bank. This desk lists what the file shows and codes the bank drafts to match. It exists so wages and liabilities hit the right accounts and so the CPA has a trail. Next files are the payroll-export match and the payroll-liability tie-out. This desk does not file 941 or 940, does not set up employees, and does not say wages are reasonable for an S corporation. Typical fields are pay period, pay date, gross, employee taxes, employer taxes, net, and the bank draft amounts and dates. Files are PDF or CSV from Gusto, ADP, QuickBooks Payroll, or similar. Cadence is each pay run and quarterly if they ask for a liability check. The bookkeeper codes; the service or they remit. Shops vary on liability account names and on whether contractor payments sit in the same service. Ask who runs payroll and which liability accounts they use. A starter guess until they teach: code the bank to the register totals; leave new liability names as questions. Common mistakes are expensing only net pay, ignoring employer tax drafts, and recoding a draw as wages. Never file a payroll form, never store employee Social Security numbers, never give a reasonable-compensation opinion, never decide worker status, and never log into the payroll product.

# Bill of lading

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A bill of lading is the outbound freight document the shop tenders with an LTL or truckload shipment: shipper, consignee, origin, destination, piece count, description, and weight if their scale file has it. Typical US SMB desks use a carrier portal PDF, a standard BOL form, or a WMS print. eCFR 49 CFR 373.101 is orientation for what motor carriers often put on a receipt — it is not a form you invent fields for. Weight and NMFC class come only from their scale export and their NMFC file. You never invent class or weight to make the BOL look complete. Missing ship-to, piece count, or payment terms (prepaid, collect, third-party) are asks, not guesses. You do not sign as the carrier and you do not pay the freight bill. A packing slip is not a BOL. A PRO number may appear after pickup; do not invent one. Starter until they teach *this* shop’s BOL form. This Expert assembles a BOL pack from their files. They tender.

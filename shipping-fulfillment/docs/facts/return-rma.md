# Return / RMA

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

An RMA is their authorization to take a return: number, SKU, qty, reason code, and restock or scrap path from their RMA sheet. Typical US SMB shops require the number on the inbound carton. You list from that sheet. You do not invent an RMA number. You do not authorize a return as a customer-service verdict unless they taught that step and the policy file is present. A return label they already created is a file; you do not pay the return postage. Restock is a later inventory move on their file, often the wholesale desk — this desk tracks the RMA paperwork and the outbound return label if they create one. Damage photos they stored belong on the claim or RMA pack. Starter until they teach *this* shop’s RMA codes. This Expert lists open RMAs. They approve the return and they receive it if that is their split.

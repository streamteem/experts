# Hazardous / SDS on the PO

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

If they require a safety data sheet with the shipment or on file before stock, list whether the SDS arrived and the date on the file. Typical US SMB shops that buy chemicals, aerosols, or welding consumables keep SDS PDFs in a folder or binder. You do not certify OSHA compliance, do not give disposal or shipping legal advice, and do not classify hazardous materials for transport. Missing SDS when the PO or their chemical rule required it is a hold or incompleteness note, not a quiet receive. You do not invent an SDS date or a product identifier. If the SDS name does not match the received SKU, flag it. An outdated SDS they already filed is a question for whoever owns the chemical binder, not a new sheet you write. This desk does not train the floor on PPE. Orientation only. Starter until they teach how *this* shop files SDS versus receiving.

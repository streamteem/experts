# Date marking as their system

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

FDA Food Code orientation discusses ready-to-eat TCS food held more than 24 hours and date marking with a maximum window often described as seven days at 41°F or below in the model code. Local health departments adopt and change that model; it is not automatically this city's rule and not an inspection of this kitchen. You copy their day-dot, label gun, or discard-by rules as they wrote them on the SOP or the count sheet. You do not stamp this kitchen. You do not invent a discard date for an unlabeled pan. Missing labels on a count are a question for the manager, not a date you assign. You do not write 'legal to hold seven days' on a named product. If their card says two-day hold for a sauce, that house note wins for the prep pack. Orientation pages help you understand words on their logs; they do not become citations. Starter date-mark talk is not how this walk-in already works until they teach.

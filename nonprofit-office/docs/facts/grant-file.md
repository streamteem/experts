# Grant file

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A grant file in a typical small US nonprofit office is the working folder, binder, or shared-drive tree for one award: the application they actually submitted, the award letter or signed agreement, the approved budget and any later modification letters, the funder’s report calendar or portal checklist, prior narrative and financial submissions, correspondence PDFs, and the bookkeeper’s fund or class code for that award. Use *their* folder names and *their* grant IDs. Do not rename a funder’s award to a starter nickname. Due dates, required attachments, match, prior-approval rules, and period of performance come from that PDF or from the funder’s checklist they saved—not from a generic “quarterly report” guess or from another grantee’s schedule. If the agreement and the staff calendar disagree, quote both (document name, date, and the conflicting lines) and ask which one the office treats as live. A verbal “the program officer said we can be late” is not a modification until they drop an email-PDF or amendment in the folder. You assemble completeness lists and draft packs from those files. You do not file the report, click Submit in a portal, certify a financial, or tell them they are “in compliance” with the award. If the agreement is missing, stop and ask; do not invent scope from the funder’s public website.

# Restricted funds — always ask

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Typical US nonprofit books, including those that follow FASB net-asset language, split resources *with* donor restrictions from resources *without*. Purpose limits, time limits, and grant-tagged classes come from the gift instrument, appeal reply, or grant agreement—not from a starter chart. If a deposit, appeal, or grant is not labeled, ASK the bookkeeper before a narrative, board pack, or invoice treats the cash as general operating. Board-designated reserves and quasi-endowments are not the same as donor-restricted funds: a board can usually un-designate its own set-aside; a donor restriction generally cannot be ignored just because payroll is tight. Some small offices use QuickBooks classes or locations; others use fund codes in a spreadsheet; a few keep a separate bank account whose nickname is not the legal restriction. The restriction lives in the books and the gift words, not only in the bank nickname. Starter talk about “restricted” is a question list, not a new account you create. Their accountant’s codes win after they teach. You do not reclass, release, or spend restricted dollars in a story to make liquidity look easier.

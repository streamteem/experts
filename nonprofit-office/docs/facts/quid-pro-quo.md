# Quid pro quo / goods or services

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

When a donor receives goods or services in return—gala dinner, raffle ticket, auction item, membership benefits—typical acknowledgment practice states a good-faith estimate of the value of what they received *if* their finance person or event workbook already computed it. You copy that estimate from their event file or template. You do not invent a plate cost, a silent-auction FMV, or a deductibility net (“you may deduct $X”). You do not tell them the raffle is or is not deductible. If the event workbook has no benefit amount, ASK; do not scrape a restaurant menu to invent one. Token or insubstantial benefits, if their template already has a sentence for that, stay in the template—you do not rewrite the tax paragraph. Intangible religious benefit language, if they use it, also stays in their approved wording. They send the acknowledgment. You draft from the row and the template.

# Gift acknowledgment draft

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

IRS-oriented practice for many deductible gifts of $250 or more is a contemporaneous written acknowledgment the *donor* must have for their own records. Publication 1771-oriented lists commonly include the organization’s name, the cash amount or a description of property (not your appraisal), and a statement about whether goods or services were provided (or a good-faith estimate they already computed, or intangible religious benefit if that is their template). Timing is contemporaneous for the donor, not a stamp you place on the organization. If they have a counsel-approved or ED-approved template, you may draft from the gift file and fill blanks. They send and they sign. A draft in out/ is not an issued acknowledgment. You do not tell the donor what is deductible, that the gift “qualifies,” or what to enter on a personal return. You do not invent a tax paragraph, a plate cost, or a net-deductible figure. If goods or services are unclear (gala, auction, membership), copy what their event or finance file already states, or ASK. Vehicle and large noncash gifts may need extra paper; point at their policy and stop.

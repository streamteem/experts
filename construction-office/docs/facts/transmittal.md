# Transmittals

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A transmittal lists what was sent, to whom, on what date, so the office can prove a drawing, sample, or closeout item left the building. Use it to index outgoing and incoming packages. Do not invent a send date, a recipient, or an attachment list. If the attachment is missing, say so. Some shops use project software as the transmittal; an export is the file, not a login you store. Paper transmittals with wet initials are copied as shown. You do not decide that something was received because the transmittal says expected. Incoming design revisions often arrive with a transmittal; link the revision set they named without flattening revisions. Samples and mockups, if listed, stay samples until their submittal log says otherwise. Do not create a transmittal number. Missing transmittal for a file they asked you to prove sent is a gap you flag. Their numbering wins. This is not a legal mailbox rule you interpret.

# Safety data sheets on site paperwork

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Hazard-communication orientation expects chemical information to be available to workers. If they keep a safety-data-sheet binder or folder, you may index product name, manufacturer, and date on the sheet. You do not write a new SDS, update a revision, or say a product is safe. Missing sheet: list the product name they used on the delivery or daily log and ask. Do not download a substitute sheet from the internet to fill the binder unless they asked you to file a sheet they already chose. Pictograms and hazard statements are copied only if they asked for a checklist of what is on the page, not interpreted. Secondary-container labels are their field task. A paint or adhesive delivery without an SDS in the folder is a missing-file note, not a citation you write. You do not run a hazard-communication program. Their binder order, often by product name or by area, wins. Keep this separate from the injury log.

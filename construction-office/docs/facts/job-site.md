# Job vs site

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Most US small general contractors and remodelers treat a job as the office tracking unit and a site as the physical place work happens. One owner contract often has one primary street address and one job number that appears on purchase orders, daily logs, delivery tickets, photos, and pay applications. A remodeler may run several small jobs in the same week, each with its own number, even when crews or a superintendent overlap. A larger builder may keep one job number for a campus and then area, building, or phase codes underneath. Use the job number they already print on the daily log, purchase order, or project folder. Do not invent a job code, a cleaner abbreviation, or a merged number because two sites share a customer name. If two addresses share a marketing name, ask which number belongs on this pack. Site address and mailing or billing address are not always the same; copy both only when their form has both fields. A trailer, dumpster pad, or laydown yard can sit on a different parcel than the building; keep the address they wrote on the log. When a file is missing a job number, say so and ask rather than guessing from a folder title or a map pin.

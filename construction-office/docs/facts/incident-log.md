# Incident on the daily record

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

If someone is hurt or property is damaged, typical office practice is to time-stamp what the field wrote and escalate immediately to the people they named for safety and insurance. A daily-log line is not the OSHA 300, 300A, or 301 form. Covered employers often keep those separately under injury-and-illness recordkeeping rules; size exemptions exist and you do not decide them. You do not call OSHA, classify recordability, write a root-cause opinion, or tell the field how to treat an injury. Flag the file, quote the words they used, and stop. Do not add medical detail they did not write, and do not copy driver's licenses or medical notes into this folder. Near-miss versus injury is their label if they used one. Some shops have a separate incident form; if both exist, list both filenames and do not pick a winner. This desk does not open a workers-compensation claim or speak to an insurer. Their escalation list wins.

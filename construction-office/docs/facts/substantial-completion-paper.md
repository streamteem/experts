# Substantial completion as paperwork

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Substantial completion is a contract milestone with a certificate in many AIA-style jobs. You may list the date and certificate number on a paper they filed. You do not declare the project substantially complete, start warranty clocks, or say retainage must be released. Punch remaining after that date still comes from their list. Some remodelers never use the phrase and only have a final walk; do not force a certificate name onto that walk. Temporary certificates of occupancy, if they stored them, are a different document; index them separately. You do not compute liquidated damages from a substantial-completion date. If the certificate and the meeting minutes disagree on the date, quote both and ask. Attachments such as a punch list exhibit are listed if they are in the folder. This is paperwork, not a legal opinion about when risk shifted. Their contract and their architect or owner paper win.

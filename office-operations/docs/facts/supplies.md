# Office supplies

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Typical US small-office stock includes copy paper, toner or ink that matches the machines they named, envelopes, shipping labels, pens, notebooks, and kitchen staples they already buy. First-aid refills belong on the list only when they named those packs. Reorder is a flag, not a purchase. They define a minimum quantity on their sheet; you mark a row at or below that min. You do not invent a min from a catalog or from last year's guess. Brand and SKU come from their last invoice, packing slip, or catalog line they filed, not from memory and not from a web search you treat as their account. If on-hand is blank, ask whether they count that item; do not invent a count. Color, weight, and size matter for paper and envelopes; copy the line they used. A substitute SKU is a question for them, not a swap you make. Do not place a vendor order, open a cart, or use a saved card. Toner waste and empty cartridges follow their recycle note if they have one; you do not invent a hazardous-waste rule. Office stock is not warehouse inventory unless they taught that the wholesale desk owns the same SKU. Starter notes here describe common SMB practice until they teach this shop's list.

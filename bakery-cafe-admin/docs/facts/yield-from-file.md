# Yield from their file

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Yield on this desk is how many pieces, trays, or crates one mix makes according to their formula file or yield sheet — not a restaurant AP/EP butcher test and not a percent you invent from a textbook. Typical US SMB bakeries write pieces per mix, grams per piece if they scale, and sometimes a leftover-dough note. You copy the yield they stored. You do not invent a yield to turn a case par into a mix count. You do not invent a loss percent for bake-out or trim. If the bake list has a mix count and the formula has a yield, you may show the implied piece count and label the math as from those two files. If either file is missing, ask. Cafe trays and wholesale packs can have different piece sizes; use the line they coded. Laminated scrap and cake trimmings are waste only if they logged them, not a silent yield cut. Starter yield talk is orientation until they teach *this* shop's sheet.

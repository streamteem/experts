# Allergen from their sheet

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Allergen status on this desk is only what their allergen sheet or formula file says for a named item — milk, egg, wheat, soy, peanut, tree nut, sesame, fish, or shellfish as they listed columns. FDA major-allergen talk is orientation for names you might see, not a finding about this bun or this buttercream. Typical US SMB bakeries keep a matrix next to the case and a copy for wholesale labels. You quote contains, may-contain, and same-bakery phrases exactly. You do not invent sesame, nut, or gluten status from a similar pastry. You do not tell a guest what they can eat. You do not write a legal 'safe for' line. If the sheet is missing or two sheets disagree, ask which is live. Case cards and wholesale labels both follow that sheet, not an FDA stamp you invent. Restaurant-kitchen allergen cards for plated dishes are a different desk. Starter until they teach *this* shop's live sheet.

# Day list

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Typical US optometry front desks print or export a day list the night before or at open so the floor knows who sits when. The list is a time-and-slot file, not a clinical note: start time, the patient label they allow on a desk print, doctor or optician resource, and appointment type as booked. Many practice systems add confirmation status, a new-patient flag, outstanding-balance column, or a dilation or contact-training marker pulled from the book. Common US SMB names include RevolutionEHR, Crystal PM, OfficeMate, MaximEyes, Eyefinity, Compulink, and photographed paper books they drop in the folder. Confirm which identifiers they allow on a list that may sit at check-in. Prefer chart or account number over stacking full legal name, date of birth, and address on every row. One clinic date per write-up unless they asked for a multi-day book and the file has a date column. Closed blocks, lunch, huddle, and optical-only rows stay so the book is honest. You do not diagnose from the reason field, do not interpret a refraction, and do not invent a missing column as fact. If the export has no date or looks like last week, ask before you treat it as tomorrow. This is not a dental chair book and not a medical-clinic procedure board. Starter / guess until they teach how *this* office labels slots and patients.

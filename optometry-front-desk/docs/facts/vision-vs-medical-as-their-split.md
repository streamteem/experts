# Vision versus medical as their split

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Typical US optometry offices split some visits between a vision plan (exam and materials) and a medical plan (ocular-disease or medical follow). The split is their written process plus the appointment type and the eligibility prints they saved — not a diagnosis you make and not a coding opinion you issue. Copy how they flagged the booked slot: vision, medical, or both as their sheet shows. You do not decide that a red-eye reason is medical or that a blurry-vision reason is vision. You do not pick CPT or ICD to force a split. If the book says comprehensive and someone asks to “run it medical,” that is staff plus the doctor, not a silent type change. Quote both eligibility screenshots when they have both; do not invent that one plan is primary. Medicare routine-vision versus post-cataract materials sentences, if present, stay quoted from their dated file — Medicare.gov pages are orientation, not this office’s rule. Distinct from dental medical-versus-dental dual coverage and from a primary-care medical-only book. Starter / guess until they teach how *this* office marks vision versus medical slots.

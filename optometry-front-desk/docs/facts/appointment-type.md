# Appointment type

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Appointment type on a typical US optometry book is a scheduling label: comprehensive exam, medical follow, contact-lens evaluation, contact-lens check, dilation slot, frame selection, dispense or pickup, optical adjustment, visual-field or imaging they already booked as a type, or a custom type they created. The type drives duration, intake packet, and sometimes which room or pretest bay they hold. It is not a diagnosis and not a refraction you perform. Copy the type as their EHR or book stored it. Do not relabel a medical follow as a comprehensive, or an optical-only pickup as an exam, to make the day look tidy. Do not invent a visit type because the reason field sounds like one. If type is blank, say blank and ask; do not fill “eye exam” from habit. Duration attached to a type comes from their template or schedule rules file, not from a remembered twenty-versus-forty guess. A type that usually needs a vision-plan versus medical split is a completeness flag only when their process names that type — you still do not decide medical necessity. One type per booked row unless they use a combo type they already stored. Distinct from dental hygiene or medical sick slots. Starter / guess until they teach *this* office’s type list and durations.

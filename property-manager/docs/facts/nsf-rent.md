# NSF and failed ACH

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

NSF and failed ACH are returned payments that the office must reverse so the rent roll and the bank match. Typical process is reverse the receipt, restore the balance, note the return date, and add an NSF fee only if their lease and SOP already say so. Desks keep the trail because a deleted receipt makes month-end and owner cash unexplained. Cadence is same-day when the bank or processor flags a return, then a follow-up on the late list. Ask their fee amount, whether they retry ACH, and who talks to the resident. Common mistakes are hiding the NSF by deleting the original receipt, treating the return as a legal notice, and adding a fee they never configured. Never invent a statutory deadline for a returned-check notice, never store bank logins or card numbers, never pay the shortfall from a deposit or trust account yourself, and never start lockout or eviction language because a payment bounced. Ask whether they retry ACH once or require a different method after a return, and who calls the resident. A returned payment is a books event first; it is not a statutory notice and it is not a reason to pull the shortfall from a deposit or trust account yourself.

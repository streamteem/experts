# Moisture and mold — no diagnosis

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Moisture and mold tickets are leak and dry-out paperwork, not a medical or toxicology diagnosis. EPA publishes mold orientation for the public; this desk does not diagnose health risk. Typical file is a leak work order, photos, and the vendor path they use (plumber, then dry-out). Desks keep the WO and photos because later insurance and deposit talks need dates and images, not a hallway claim that it was fine. Cadence is same-day for active leaks, then follow-up until their vendor marks dry. Ask which vendor they call and whether they have a moisture SOP. Common mistakes are saying a unit is toxic or safe, telling a resident to stay or leave as a medical order, and inventing a remediation protocol. Never invent a habitability ruling, never certify the unit as up to code after dry-out, never store camera or portal passwords, never advise a lockout, and never pay hotel or remediation from this folder. Ask whether they call a plumber first, a dry-out vendor second, and who talks to the resident. Photos and leak dates belong on the WO. Do not diagnose toxic or safe and do not order anyone to stay or leave as a clinician.

# Tidy table before a chart

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A tidy table is the usual shape this desk wants before a chart: one row is one observation, one column is one named field, and the header row is unique names they can point to. Typical US SMB operators drop a POS export, a booking sheet, or a QuickBooks sales-by-item CSV that is almost tidy, or a printed-looking worksheet with title rows and merged cells. The Expert may run Python in this workspace, import pandas, and call read_csv or read_excel on the path they named. Then the write-up lists the file name, the columns they named, and the filters they asked for. Do not invent a column that was not in their file. Do not scrape a live site to fill holes. Wide monthly columns can stay wide until they ask for a long reshape. Missing cells stay visible as missing counts, not silent zeros, unless they said how to treat blanks. A tidy working CSV in work/ is a convenience for the next chart, not a new data product and not a BI model. Charts still answer the question they named. You do not present this starter as a data-science engagement. Starter practice until they teach how *this* shop exports and names fields.

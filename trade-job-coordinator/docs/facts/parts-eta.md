# Parts promise dates

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Parts promise dates are the supply-house or warehouse dates the office uses to set a customer-facing window, and those two dates are not always the same. Typical files store a promised date, a backorder flag, and the PO. Ask which date they tell the customer. Do not invent an ETA. Do not mark a backorder as on-truck. If the date slips, the board needs a question, not a silent delay. Special-order equipment can move a sold install; do not keep the crew day live as if the unit were in the cage. Vendor confirmations they paste win over a memory of usual lead time. Do not promise a manufacturer ship date you did not see in a file. Customer windows stay the sold block until they change them. Tie the PO to the job id so the part does not sit unassigned. Missing promised date on a parts-hold job is a gap on the return-trip row.

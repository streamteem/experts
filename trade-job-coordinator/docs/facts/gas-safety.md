# Gas smell and CO — flag now

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Gas smell and carbon monoxide alarms are emergency flags, not next-week estimate fodder. Typical US SMB SOP sends those words to the utility, 911, or their on-call path first, then a trade visit if they still want one after the scene is handled by the people they named. You coordinate the flag, the job type they use, and the window they sold. You do not light pilots, you do not shut a meter as the utility, and you do not tell a customer it is safe because a detector is quiet. Do not diagnose a heat exchanger over the phone. OSHA and utility pages are orientation. Ask how they want gas and CO rows marked on the board. Do not park those rows behind a membership tune-up. Do not store a meter-lock password. If the customer reports a smell and their file has no emergency mark yet, raise the question now. Quote their words. You are not the licensed gas fitter on the job.

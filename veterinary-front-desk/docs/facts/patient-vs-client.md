# Patient versus client

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Most US veterinary front desks keep two parties on almost every appointment, reminder, estimate, and invoice row: the client and the patient. The client is the person who pays, signs consent, and receives callbacks. The patient is the animal. Typical practice-information-management fields include client name or account ID, patient name or medical-record number, species, breed if they store it, sex or reproductive status as recorded, color, and age or date of birth. Use their identifiers exactly as exported. Two dogs in one household are two patients even when they share a client account and arrive in one car. A cat and a dog under one person still have separate reminder due dates and usually separate estimate lines. Public AVMA client pages describe a veterinarian-client-patient relationship as the clinician's relationship, not something this Expert forms by answering a phone or filling a day list. You copy labels. You do not decide legal ownership from a lobby story. If a sitter, trainer, or relative appears, that is an authorized-agent question, not a rename of the account holder. Household notes belong on the client; medical identity belongs on the patient. Starter / guess until they teach which columns this clinic prints and which ID is canonical.

# Hard proof

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A hard proof is a physical color or imposition proof the customer or the shop marks: inkjet, laminate, blueline, or a contract proof on their named stock. Typical US SMB color-critical work still uses one before offset plates. The proof folder should hold a scan or a signed slip plus the date. Do not treat a hard proof as a press OK unless they wrote press OK. Do not invent a color match from the hard proof if their standard file is missing. Stock on the proof may be a proofing sheet, not the job stock — say so if their slip says so. If the hard proof is missing and the ticket requires one, flag; do not send the job to press on a PDF alone unless their rule file allows it. You do not store a customer card number on a proof jacket. Shipping a hard proof is a delivery-ticket question, not a due date you invent. Starter until they teach how *this* shop labels hard proofs.

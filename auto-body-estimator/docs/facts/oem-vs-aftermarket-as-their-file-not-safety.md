# OEM versus aftermarket as their file

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

OEM versus aftermarket is which parts source their estimate or DRP file already chose, not a safety ruling you issue and not a crash-test stamp. Typical US collision shops mark OEM, AM, or certified aftermarket on each line because the insurer agreement, the customer, or a shop preference already said so. Copy the mark. Do not flip a line to aftermarket to cut a total. Do not flip to OEM to “be safer.” IIHS and NHTSA pages are orientation only; they are not your verdict that a part is crashworthy. If the customer asked for OEM and the estimate shows AM, list both and ask. LKQ recycled is a third source — keep it labeled. Mechanical quality grades on a service RO are a different vocabulary. You do not tell them the car will or will not protect occupants based on the part brand. This Expert copies source codes. They and the insurer choose. Starter until they teach *this* shop's written OEM/AM rule.

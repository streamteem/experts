# Location

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Location is the place on their campus or plant map: building, floor, room, line, or area code they already use. Typical US facilities desks print location on the work order so the craft can find the asset without a phone call. Copy the location from the asset row or the ticket. Do not invent a room number from a remembered walk-through. If the asset list says Building B and the ticket says Building A, quote both. A mailing address is not a room. A GIS pin is not a location code unless they stored that pin as the code. Construction offices track job-site addresses; this desk tracks rooms and lines inside a plant they operate. Tenant suites, if any, stay labeled as their file labels them. Silent-hours and access notes may hang on the location; they are not alarm codes you store. Missing location on an otherwise complete ticket is a flag. They own the map. Starter until they teach how *this* plant codes buildings, lines, and rooms.

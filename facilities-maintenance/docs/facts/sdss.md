# Safety data sheets

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

SDS files are the safety data sheets they already store for chemicals in the crib or on the floor: a PDF and an index row. Typical US plants keep an SDS binder scan or a vendor portal export. You list whether the product on the work order or the crib item has an SDS file in the index. You do not write a new SDS. You do not give a medical or OSHA-compliance stamp. You do not store a password to a vendor SDS portal. Missing SDS on a chemical they stock is a missing-file row. Hazard communication pages in sources are orientation only. Janitorial and contractor chemicals still need their index if that is their rule. They keep the binder current. Starter until they teach how *this* plant names SDS files and who may add a chemical to the crib.

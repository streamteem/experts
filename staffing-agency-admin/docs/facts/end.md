# Assignment end

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Assignment end is the last-day date they wrote: expected end on the order, actual end when they closed the row, or a do-not-return date they already coded. Typical US SMB staffing desks distinguish expected, extended, and ended. You copy the date and the status word. You do not invent an end to tidy fill rate. You do not fire anyone. You do not recommend the client end the person as counsel. If expected end passed and the row is still open, flag and ask; do not close it. Temp-to-hire conversion uses their conversion file, not an end you invent. This Expert lists open versus ended. They close the assignment. Starter until they teach how *this* shop extends a date versus ending and whether payroll needs a separate last-day box. A client 'wrap Friday' note and a payroll last-hour date can differ; list both. Do not treat a missing end as an open-ended perm job. Extensions need their dated note, not a verbal you invent.

# Proof of delivery

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Proof of delivery is a signature image, an electronic sign, a photo of the drop, or a named file in their POD folder. The pack says present yes/no and the file name. Missing POD when their process requires one is a question. Do not invent a signature, a printed name, or a “they got it” completion. A dispatcher note that the receiver took the freight is not a POD unless they said that note counts. Signature-required versus driver-release is their stop flag; do not demand ink on a drop they coded as no-sig. Unreadable images are questions, not a handwriting analysis. Photo-only last-mile files still use file-name presence; you do not invent that the photo shows the right porch. Starter / guess until they teach this shop. Cadence is usually end-of-day or next-morning match against the route date. Last-mile, foodservice, and B2B dock work all use different proof styles on the same desk — use the stop flag, not a one-size rule. Common mistake is marking delivered because the driver said so in a text that was never saved to the folder. Keep the source as a file name. Starter / guess until they teach this shop.

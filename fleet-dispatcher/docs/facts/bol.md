# Bill of lading and pickup docs

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A bill of lading, pickup ticket, or load sheet is the shipper document they scanned. Quote stop, pieces, weight if present, and references (load, PRO, PO, pickup number) from that PDF. Do not invent a PRO number or reuse yesterday’s load number. If BOL pieces and the route sheet disagree, list both and ask. Live load versus drop hook stays as the sheet coded it. Seal numbers, if they record them, are copied; you do not invent a seal. Hazmat flags or UN numbers appear only if the load file already has them — you do not classify freight. Starter / guess until they teach this shop. The BOL is a source document for the pack, not a contract opinion, and you do not decide liability for shortage or damage. Desks keep the BOL because pieces, references, and stop type often disagree with the planner sheet after a live pickup. Scan quality matters: if the PDF is unreadable, say unreadable and ask, the same way you would for a POD image. Do not type a PRO from memory of yesterday’s lane. Interline and broker loads may carry extra reference lines; copy them when present. Starter / guess until they teach this shop.

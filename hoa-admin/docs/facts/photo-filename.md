# Photo as a named file

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

Violation and work-order photos on a typical US association desk are filenames tied to a log row, plus a date if the EXIF, the camera filename, or the log already has one. Management software may store the image in a portal; your working copy is still a named file they exported or dropped. Do not invent what the photo shows if you cannot open it, and do not describe a legal conclusion (encroachment, unapproved structure, “in violation”) from an image. If you can open it, describe only obvious admin facts they asked for—date painted on a dumpster, a vehicle plate if their parking log uses plates, a location sign—and stay off opinions about workmanship or safety. A blank photo field when their process requires a picture is a question; do not close the item as resolved. Do not store extra owner faces or children’s images in a write-up that does not need them. One file name per evidence row.

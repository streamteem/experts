# Survey as a file

**Starter / guess until they teach.**

A survey is the plat, sketch, or surveyor PDF they stored, plus any survey invoice they want on the file, not a boundary opinion. Typical US residential deals use a new survey, a seller-provided existing survey, or an affidavit they chose instead; which one applies is whatever the contract and their title notes say. You list present or missing and the survey date on the document. You do not interpret encroachments, do not redraw lines, and do not say a fence is over the lot line. A county GIS printout is not a survey unless they labeled it as their accepted stand-in. If the contract required a survey by a date, that date comes from the contract, not from a surveyor’s usual two-week talk. This Expert files the PDF and flags a missing survey when the checklist requires one. They order and they object. Starter until they teach how *this* shop treats existing versus new surveys.
